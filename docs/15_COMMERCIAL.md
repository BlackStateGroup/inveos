# 15 — Commercial proposition and go-to-market hypotheses

## Customer choice

Test **corporate due diligence and internal investigations** as the first market hypothesis, including professional agencies doing that work. The supplied-document supplier case is the first demonstration target. Observe current assignment-to-report workflows, interview five to ten prospective users and recruit a small pilot group before fixing the offer or pricing. This supersedes the foundation's agency-first assumption as a research direction; no demand, interviews, customer commitments or willingness to pay are claimed. Keep the architecture suitable for other lawful investigation types.

Use pilot evidence to choose templates, onboarding and support. Measure storage, processing, retention and support costs before promising allowances; the broader commercial scope remains gated on privacy/security and operational readiness. See [WP-18 and the delivery plan](24_DELIVERY_PLAN.md).

## Pricing experiments (not researched willingness-to-pay)

- Field Solo: **£29–£49/user/month** with storage/processing allowance.
- Investigator Professional: **£79–£129/user/month** with case and reporting tools.
- Agency Command: **£199–£399/agency/month** entry package plus field seats/usage where justified.
- Enterprise/on-premises: contract price after security, deployment and support scope.

Do not announce these as validated market rates. Test whether agencies will pay for report turnaround time, secure field workflows, evidence/custody audit and portal delivery.

## Competitive baseline

Case management and administration exist already (CROSStrax, Trackops). Mature cyber-investigation/intelligence platforms (OpenCTI, TheHive, DFIR-IRIS, MISP) show the importance of collaboration, audit and integrations but are not automatically suitable PI products. INVEOS must compete on a trustworthy end-to-end professional investigation workflow, ease of use and verifiable evidence references, not on a claim to have invented case software or AI.

## Pilot interview questions

1. What is the current workflow from instruction to final report? Show the actual tools used.
2. What tasks consume the most paid investigator time? What creates mistakes?
3. How do you record and prove when a photo or statement was captured?
4. What happens when an investigator has no signal, loses their phone or retries uploads?
5. How do you identify unsupported claims and keep client disclosures restricted?
6. Which legal/retention and client security questions block vendor adoption?
7. What would a one-week pilot have to deliver to justify a subscription?
8. What existing software would this replace, and what would stop a migration?

## Commercial tests

- Ten target accounts interviewed; five willing to use a synthetic-data demo; three agreed to a time-boxed privacy-approved pilot; one paying customer only after compliance readiness.
- Measure weekly active investigators, cases actively worked, minutes saved per report and case completion, customer retention and support burden.
- Acquisition partnerships: ABI-linked professional networks and legal-support providers, subject to association's endorsement rules and no implied certification.
